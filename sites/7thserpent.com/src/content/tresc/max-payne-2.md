  - id: story
    year: The story
    title: Two years later, the same city
    meta: The setup and the cast — the ending is left for further down
    art: mp2-k01
    body:
      - >-
        Max has been acquitted of the charges that hung over him after the
        first game, and he is a detective with the NYPD again — his connections
        to the Inner Circle, a secret society, got him the badge back. He still
        has nightmares about the past. Max tells the story in flashback, and the
        first one puts him in a hospital bed, recovering from a bullet, before
        the game rewinds a few more nights.
      - >-
        It starts with a shootout at a warehouse that belongs to Vladimir Lem,
        a Russian mobster and Max’s friend, where a gang of hitmen called the
        Cleaners has come for the guns. There Max runs into Mona Sax, the
        contract killer he believed had died two years earlier. From that night
        the trail runs through Vinnie Gognitti, now underboss of the
        Punchinello family, and Max’s colleague Valerie Winterson, all the way
        up to Senator Alfred Woden, the head of the Inner Circle.
  - id: noir-love-story
    year: The writing
    title: A film noir love story
    meta: Sam Lake’s script for the sequel
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Sam Lake, who wrote the first game and gave Max his face in it, wrote
        this one as well and decided on a film noir love story, which he felt
        suited Max better than anything else. The first game had drawn on Norse
        mythology and hard-boiled detective novels; the sequel borrows from the
        doomed romances of noir cinema and puts Max and Mona at its center.
      - >-
        Lake’s plan for the sequel was to hold on to what had worked, repair
        what had not and then steer the plot somewhere players would not see
        coming. His script ended up three times the length of the first one,
        and he hoped the more tangled story would pay off on a second run.
        He also argued that the genre’s stock elements — a hostile city full of
        crime, heavy rain late at night, a cynical detective down on his luck —
        still carry a story a long way.
  - id: gameplay
    year: Gameplay
    title: Three parts, two playable heroes
    meta: How the campaign is built and what it unlocks
    body:
      - >-
        The campaign is linear and split into three parts, each made up of
        several chapters. Most of it puts you in Max’s shoes, but in several
        levels of parts two and three you play as Mona. You start with a 9mm pistol and work up to
        shotguns, submachine guns, assault rifles and sniper rifles, and a
        grenade or a Molotov cocktail now sits in its own secondary slot. Up
        close, either hero can pistol-whip an enemy.
      - >-
        Max’s inner monologue tells you what to do next. On a first playthrough
        there is only one difficulty, and it adjusts itself: die too often and
        the enemies get less sharp while painkillers turn up more often.
        Beating the game unlocks the harder settings and two modes — New York
        Minute, which scores each level by how quickly you clear it, and Dead
        Man Walking, five set pieces where you hold out as long as you can
        against enemies that keep respawning.
  - id: bullet-time
    year: Bullet time
    title: Bullet time, version 2.0
    meta: What changed in the slow motion
    art: mp2-k02
    flip: true
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        The slow motion is back: time crawls while your aim keeps full speed,
        and the picture turns sepia so you know it is running. A meter empties
        while you use it and fills back up on its own when you stop — or much
        faster if you kill enemies. A shootdodge, a dive in any direction you
        choose, slows time in mid-air and costs nothing from the meter.
      - >-
        Remedy called the new system version 2.0. Each kill in a streak slows
        time a little more and returns part of the meter, a change meant to
        get players diving into fights head-first instead of waiting behind
        cover. Reloading in bullet time now plays its own animation: Max ducks,
        spins to look around the room and holds still for a beat, which buys
        you a second to plan the next move.
  - id: physics
    year: Engine
    title: Havok physics and toppling cover
    meta: The technology behind the firefights
    art: mp2-k00
    body:
      - >-
        Max Payne 2 runs on an upgraded version of the first game’s engine
        with the Havok physics engine added on top — the developers said it
        was plainly the best fit for what they needed. Crates you crouch
        behind tip over when enemy fire hits them, a grenade thrown next to a
        wall can send an enemy’s cover flying, and bodies land convincingly
        after an explosion. Weapons, bombs and Molotovs were tuned to behave
        more naturally, and their sound was redone to match.
      - >-
        The game supports only DirectX 8.1, yet its graphics imitate DirectX 9
        by making the most of effects such as reflections, refraction, shaders
        and ghosting; the developers singled out Max’s lucid dreams, where the
        whole screen goes blurry and soft. Characters have more polygons than before, so
        their outlines look smoother, and far more facial expressions: in the
        first game Max had just one, here he smirks and raises his eyebrows.
  - id: cast
    year: Cast
    title: A new face, the same voice
    meta: Who plays whom, and how the story is shown
    art: mp2-k03
    flip: true
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Max looks different: the first game modeled him on Sam Lake, the
        sequel on the actor Timothy Gibbs. James McCaffrey voices Max again,
        and the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences nominated him for its
        award for outstanding male character performance. Also in the cast:
        Wendy Hoopes as Mona, Jonathan Davis as Vlad, Fred Berman as Vinnie,
        John Braden as Senator Woden and Jennifer Server as Winterson.
      - >-
        Comic-book panels with voice-over still frame the story, though more
        scenes than before play out in the engine. The developers kept the
        panels on purpose: they cost less than full animation and worked better,
        they could be reshuffled whenever the plot changed during development,
        and they leave part of each scene to the player’s imagination.
  - id: music
    year: Music
    title: Late Goodbye
    meta: The score and the song
    body:
      - >-
        Kärtsy Hatakka and Kimmo Kajasto came back for the score, with cello
        played by Perttu Kivilaakso of Apocalyptica. The end credits roll over
        Late Goodbye by Poets of the Fall, a song whose lyrics grew out of a
        poem by Sam Lake.
      - >-
        Late Goodbye was the band’s first single. It climbed to number 14 on
        the Finnish singles chart and to number 1 on Radio Suomipop’s Top 30,
        and inside the game you hear it again and again, sung or hummed by the
        characters.
  - id: endings
    year: Spoilers
    title: How it ends — both ways
    meta: Skip this row if you have not finished the game
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Woden tells Max that the Cleaners answer to Vlad, who wants the Inner
        Circle and the city’s underworld for himself; Winterson turns out to
        have been Vlad’s lover and helper. Vlad kills Vinnie,
        shoots Max and reveals that Mona has been working for Woden — and that
        it was Woden who sent the Valhalla file to the district attorney’s
        office, the file Max’s wife stumbled on before his family was killed.
      - >-
        At Woden’s mansion Mona turns on Max to carry out her orders and cannot
        go through with it; Vlad shoots her and kills Woden, and Max kills Vlad
        as the house burns around them. In the standard ending Mona dies in
        Max’s arms as the police arrive. Finish
        the game on the highest difficulty and you get the other ending, in
        which Mona lives and the two of them kiss.
  - id: release
    year: Release
    title: October 2003 on PC
    meta: The deal, the date and the modding tools
    body:
      - >-
        Take-Two bought the Max Payne series from Remedy and 3D Realms on
        December 5, 2001, paying $10 million plus 970,000 shares of stock, and said
        in the same announcement that a sequel was coming. In May 2002 it
        agreed to pay the two companies up to $8 million more in incentives
        for making it, and in September 2003 it set the date: October 15.
      - >-
        Max Payne 2: The Fall of Max Payne came out on Windows on October 15,
        2003 (October 24 in Europe). Rockstar and Remedy also put out
        development tools, so players could build mods with new weapons, moves,
        camera views, places and characters.
  - id: consoles
    year: PS2 and Xbox
    title: The console versions
    meta: Xbox on November 25, 2003, PS2 on December 2 (both December 5 in Europe)
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Remedy made the game itself; Rockstar Vienna ported it to Xbox and
        PlayStation 2 within seven weeks of the PC release. Metacritic rates
        the Xbox version 84 and the PS2 one 73 — the only one of the three to
        land in mixed territory.
      - >-
        A New Zealand Herald reviewer praised the Xbox port for looking close
        to the PC game, but found the PS2 version only an okay job, noticeably
        rougher on the eye and outclassed by the stronger machines.
  - id: reviews
    year: Reviews
    title: Critics loved it, and wanted more of it
    meta: Metacritic — PC 86, Xbox 84, PS2 73
    body:
      - >-
        Reviews were warm: Metacritic puts the PC version at 86. IGN scored the
        PC game 9.4 and GameSpot 9.0. Critics praised the action and the story,
        and the main complaint was that it ends too soon.
      - >-
        IGN’s Tom McNamara felt the noir setting gave Max and Mona’s story
        real weight, though some lines struck him as hammy. The Sunday Times
        was the harshest, with 2 out of 5 and a verdict that the story was
        cheesy.
      - >-
        The Toronto Sun’s Steve Tilley called the lifelike physics the game’s
        best feature but wished it were less linear, and the Sydney Morning
        Herald’s Dan Toose, who also found the campaign short, felt the harder
        settings and the extra modes give it replay value. 1UP’s complaint was
        different: the final boss fight, it said, is a weak close to an
        otherwise excellent game.
      - >-
        It collected several awards: Outstanding Art Direction at the 2004
        Golden Satellite Awards, Editors’ Choice from GamePro, IGN, GameSpy and
        GameSpot, Game of the Month from Game Informer, and five nominations
        at the 7th Interactive Achievement Awards, Game of the Year among them.
  - id: sales
    year: Sales
    title: A hit with critics, a miss in stores
    meta: What Take-Two said about it
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Good reviews did not turn into sales. In early 2004 Take-Two named the
        “continued disappointing sales” of Max Payne 2 as one reason it cut its
        forecast for the year, and by June 2005 the game had sold only 950,000
        copies in the US. After it, Remedy went on to Alan Wake, and the series
        waited nearly a decade for Max Payne 3.
  - id: length
    year: Length
    title: About six hours
    meta: HowLongToBeat, September 2026
    body:
      - >-
        As of September 2026, HowLongToBeat puts the main story at about 6
        hours, with extras at about 7½ and everything at about 9½ — shorter
        than the first game; reviewers in 2003 already complained that it ends
        too soon. For a second run there are the unlockable difficulty levels
        and the two extra modes.
  - id: where-to-play
    year: Today
    title: Where to play it today
    meta: Official stores only, as of September 2026
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        On PC, the game has been on Steam since January 4, 2008, published by
        Rockstar Games. As of September 2026 about 94 percent of its roughly
        14,700 Steam reviews are positive. On Steam Deck, Valve rates it
        Playable: the default controls work, though the game sometimes shows
        keyboard or other controller icons and does not switch to an external
        pad by itself.
      - >-
        On Xbox, as of September 2026, the store lists Max Payne 2 for Xbox One
        and Xbox Series X|S — the whole trilogy has been backward compatible
        there since November 15, 2021 — rated Mature 17+ for intense violence,
        blood, sexual themes and strong language.
      - >-
        Getting the PC version to start can take some work. A player guide
        from around 2017 says the Steam copies of the first two games would not
        start on Windows 7 and later. Its fix: Windows 98/ME compatibility mode
        on the game’s executable (running it as administrator is recommended
        too), a game folder renamed without spaces, the matching installdir
        line in the game’s Steam app manifest changed to the new name, and a
        restart of Steam. As of September 2026, Steam’s community hub also
        lists player guides for a widescreen fix on Windows 10 and for startup
        problems on modern PCs.
