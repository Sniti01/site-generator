verdict:
  label: Verdict
  body:
    - >-
      Mirage is for players who found that Origins, Odyssey and Valhalla took
      too much of their time, and who missed the city, the rooftops and the
      quiet kill with the hood up. Baghdad is the best thing here: dense,
      alive, with four districts to explore and the option to play in Arabic.
      The main story wraps up in fifteen to twenty hours, and completing
      everything takes about thirty — for someone tired of the series, that
      is a strength, not a flaw.
    - >-
      Anyone looking for a strong story will get less than the opening
      promises. Stealth
      and parkour work, combat is an add-on, and the investigation–target
      pattern repeats to the very end. If you know Basim from Valhalla, you
      will see where he came from; if you don’t, you get a self-contained
      story with an ending you will only fully understand after Valhalla.

    body:
      - >-
        Assassin’s Creed Mirage came out on October 5, 2023 on Windows,
        PlayStation 4 and 5, Xbox One and Series, and in the cloud on Amazon
        Luna; on June 6, 2024 a version for the iPhone 15 Pro and compatible
        iPads followed. It is the thirteenth main-line game, the successor to
        Valhalla and the predecessor of Shadows. It was made by Ubisoft
        Bordeaux — a studio that had until then supported other teams and
        built the Wrath of the Druids expansion for Valhalla, and here led an
        entire production for the first time.
      - >-
        The project began as another large Valhalla expansion and only grew
        into a standalone game during development; before the announcement
        it went by the codename Rift. Hence the scale: one city and its
        surroundings instead of a whole country, and a campaign of fifteen to
        twenty hours. The RPG elements of Origins, Odyssey and Valhalla were
        set aside in favor of stealth, parkour and assassinations.
      - >-
        The protagonist is Basim ibn Is’haq — a master Assassin in Valhalla,
        here a street thief whom the Hidden Ones first turn away and then
        train. The story begins in 861, during unrest in the Abbasid
        Caliphate, and the modern-day story carries on from Valhalla.

    body:
      - >-
        In 861 Basim steals on the streets of Anbar with his childhood friend
        Nehal and has been dreaming of a djinn for years. He wants to join
        the Hidden Ones, but Roshan, a master of the Brotherhood, refuses. To
        prove himself, he breaks into the palace with Nehal for a chest that
        both the Hidden Ones and the Order of the Ancients are after. Caliph
        al-Mutawakkil catches him — Nehal kills the ruler. The guards take
        revenge on the city’s thieves, Basim blames Nehal and leaves, and
        Roshan takes him to Alamut, the Hidden Ones’ fortress.
      - >-
        After training and initiation, Basim sets out with his mentor for
        Baghdad, where the Order has taken the top posts of the caliphate and
        the caliph is its puppet. He uncovers and eliminates one member after
        another until he stands before their leader, Qabiha, who promises
        answers in a temple beneath Alamut; Roshan kills her and forbids any
        further search. Basim goes against his mentor, and in the temple he
        learns who he really is — Valhalla players know the answer: Nehal and
        the djinn were part of his own memory as Loki of the Isu.
  - id: bagdad
    year: Baghdad
    title: Four districts, Anbar and Alamut — a city instead of a country
    meta: Round City · Karkh · Abbasiyah · Harbiyah · House of Wisdom
    art: foto-samarra
    flip: false
    band: false
    body:
      - >-
        The city has four districts: the Round City, industrial Karkh,
        Harbiyah, and Abbasiyah, home to the House of Wisdom. Beyond the walls
        lie several settlements, among them Anbar, and the main story also
        leads to Alamut — in this era the Hidden Ones’ fortress is still
        under construction. In scale Baghdad resembles Paris in Unity or
        Constantinople in Revelations, that is, a single region from the last
        three installments. The real 9th-century Baghdad did not survive the
        Mongol invasion of 1258, so the team worked from surviving plans and
        written sources from that century.
      - >-
        The historical figures include Ali ibn Muhammad, leader of the Zanj
        Rebellion, the Banu Musa brothers, and Caliph al-Mu’tazz with his
        mother Qabiha. The role of the database from earlier games is filled
        by the History of Baghdad mode: sixty-six sites with descriptions by
        specialists in Islamic art. It is also the first game with Arabic
        voice acting; Eurogamer praised the details in the background, such
        as the call to prayer heard across the city and the option to mute
        the music during salat.
  - id: skradanie-i-parkour
    year: Stealth
    title: Hidden blade, Enkidu, poles and Assassin’s Focus
    meta: Black box from Unity · Eagle Vision · up to five targets at once
    art: assassins-creed-mirage-k06
    flip: true
    band: false
    body:
      - >-
        The core is parkour, stealth and melee combat — in that order. Basim’s
        arsenal includes the hidden blade, throwing knives, sleep darts and
        smoke bombs; weapons and tools can be upgraded. Tricks from the first
        games return: blending into crowds, the bench, hiding in plain sight.
        Contracts on key targets follow the black box model of Unity and
        Syndicate: the player scouts the area and picks the route to the kill.
      - >-
        Scouting is done with Enkidu the eagle, with one difference from
        earlier games with a scouting bird: archers at set positions shoot at
        it. New is Assassin’s Focus: a meter charges with stealth kills, and
        on activation time stops, the player marks up to five enemies and
        Basim takes them out one after another. The second novelty is poles,
        used to cross gaps between rooftops. Eurogamer praised the balance
        that discourages open fights and the options to turn off hints.

    year: 2020–2023
    title: A Valhalla prequel and a tribute to the first game
    meta: The series’ fifteenth anniversary · Hidden Ones and Order of the Ancients · modern-day story
    art: assassins-creed-valhalla
    flip: false
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        Mirage is set about a decade before Valhalla and tells how Basim
        became the man the player met in Viking England. The modern-day story
        continues after Valhalla’s final chapter: present-day Assassins relive
        Basim’s memories from a sample of his blood.
      - >-
        The game was made for the series’ fifteenth anniversary, on
        Valhalla’s technology, as a nod to the first Assassin’s Creed: hence
        Alamut, the cradle of the Brotherhood, and the investigation model
        with targets to track down. The Brotherhood is still called the
        Hidden Ones here, and its enemies the Order of the Ancients, as in
        Origins. The same pattern — identify a member of the Order, carry out
        the sentence — had already driven the three previous games.

        Before Ubisoft announced anything, the game was talked about as Rift:
        Jason Schreier of Bloomberg reported that the next Assassin’s Creed

        game. The announcement came on September 10, 2022 at Ubisoft Forward.
        Release was planned for October 12, 2023 and moved a week earlier.
      - >-
        Updates came for free: in December 2023 New Game+ and Bayek’s outfit
        from Origins, in February 2024 a one-life full synchronization mode.
        Director Stéphane Boudon said before launch that there would be no
        DLC, and yet on November 18, 2025 Ubisoft released the Valley of
        Memory for free — the AlUla region, a story about Basim’s missing
        father, about six hours — together with new difficulty levels,
        improved parkour and mission replays with challenges.

    year: Start
    title: Who Mirage is for and where in the series to play it
    meta: 15–20 hours for the main story · about 30 for everything · after Valhalla or as your first
    art: assassins-creed-mirage-k00
    flip: true
    band: false
    body:
      - >-
        Mirage gives the most to someone who liked the series from the first
        game to Syndicate and grew tired of three games in which it’s easy to
        sink a hundred hours: they get one city, targets to track down and
        quiet elimination as the main way to play. The second audience is the
        Valhalla player who wants to know where Basim came from. The third —
        anyone drawn to golden-age Baghdad and a historical mode to read.
      - >-
        You can start Mirage from scratch: the story is self-contained and
        the present day is barely sketched. The ending lands more fully after
        Valhalla, though, so the natural order is Origins, Odyssey, Valhalla,
        Mirage — as the where-to-start guide advises. If you want a big world
        for the long haul, there’s Valhalla or Shadows; if you want something
        short and old-school, this is it.
      - >-
        The main story takes fifteen to twenty hours, completing everything
        about thirty. Metacritic settled at 76–77 points depending on the
        platform, and IGN gave it 8 out of 10. Commercially it did well: in
        November 2025 Ubisoft reported ten million players.
