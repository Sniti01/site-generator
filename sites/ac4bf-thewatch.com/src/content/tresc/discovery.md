      - >-
        Assassin’s Creed II: Discovery came out on the Nintendo DS on
        November 17, 2009, the same day as II on the home consoles; the
        iPhone version reached the App Store on January 7, 2010. The game
        was made by Griptonite Games, the studio behind Bloodlines on the
        PSP.
      - >-
        Story-wise it is an offshoot of II, squeezed in between its
        sequences 12 and 13, that is, between the Battle of Forlì and the
        Bonfire of the Vanities. In 1491 Ezio breaks off his pursuit of the
        Apple, which Savonarola has stolen, and travels to Spain, where
        Templars operating under the banner of the Inquisition are capturing
        Assassins.

        In Venice, Luis de Santángel asks Ezio to protect Christopher
        Columbus from an assassination attempt; Ezio agrees when he hears of
        the sailor’s ties to Rodrigo Borgia. Most of the Spanish Brotherhood
        is already in the dungeons of Tomás de Torquemada: in Barcelona Ezio
        saves an Assassin from the stake, in Zaragoza he frees more
        prisoners and discovers that the orders come from Borgia himself.
      - >-
        In Granada the Templars hold the local king, Muhammad, captive so
        that the siege of the Alhambra keeps draining Queen Isabella’s
        treasury; Ezio frees the ruler. Columbus sets off for Paris to seek
        money from Charles VIII — Ezio saves him a second time and brings
        him back. When the expedition sets sail, Torquemada slips away from
        the Assassin, and Ezio admits that the Grand Inquisitor may not have
        belonged to the order at all.

    body:
      - >-
        The graphics are three-dimensional, but the camera looks from the
        side, and the path through each level is laid out in advance.
        Halberdiers, captains and archers can be avoided or killed with the
        blade, sword and knives, quietly from a ledge, a haystack or a
        barrel; combat has counters and clashes with a strength bar.
      - >-
        Memories come in three kinds: standard ones, chases under a hail of
        arrows, and stealth sections where you may be spotted no more than
        three times. Each has optional objectives for full synchronization,
        and synchronization points unlock Animus hacks — outfits and aids.
        On the DSi and iPhone, the camera puts the player’s face on wanted
        posters.

    meta: Announced at an Apple event · secondhand today · after II or not at all
    art: foto-nintendo-ds

    body:
      - >-
        The game was revealed on September 9, 2009, at an Apple event as an
        iPhone title; Ubisoft added the DS version at the end of September,
        and in 2013 the game disappeared from the App Store. What remains is
        the DS cartridge, bought secondhand. Critics saw Discovery as a step
        forward from Altaïr’s Chronicles from 2008: they praised the side
        view and the animation, and criticized the bland missions and
        awkward controls.
      - >-
        Discovery makes sense for someone who has finished II, has a
        Nintendo DS at hand and wants to see Ezio’s Spanish chapter. If you
        are starting the series from scratch, go in through II and leave
        Discovery for later or skip it altogether, like Bloodlines and
        Pirates — Brotherhood begins where II ended, with or without the
        Spanish expedition. The series would return to the side view in the
        Chronicles trilogy of 2015–2016.
