verdict:
  label: Verdict
  body:
    - >-
      The best starting point for someone who wants to see what Assassin’s
      Creed was before it became an RPG: a city instead of a continent,
      rooftops instead of a horse, a hero you remember by name. Ezio’s story
      keeps its pace from the execution to the Vatican.
    - >-
      You have to make allowances for its age: stiff animations, simple
      combat, climbing that sometimes veers off where the player didn’t
      want to go. The remaster improved the picture, not the controls. If
      you can’t put up with that, there is Mirage from 2023 — the same
      formula in a new look.
    - >-
      And one more thing: II ends in the middle of the story. When you plan
      it, plan Brotherhood right away — it is one story in three parts.

    body:
      - >-
        Assassin’s Creed II came out on November 17, 2009 on PlayStation 3
        and Xbox 360, and on PC on March 4, 2010. The game comes from the
        same Montreal studio of Ubisoft that had made the first game two
        years earlier. What remains of the shared story is the modern-day
        thread and the Animus; the era, the cities and the hero are new.
      - >-
        The action takes place in 1476–1499, 285 years after Altaïr. The
        hero is Ezio Auditore da Firenze, the son of a Florentine banker,
        who at the start knows nothing about the Assassins and spends the
        first hours on brawls and girls. The player learns the craft
        together with him instead of being handed a ready-made master.
      - >-
        The map is several separate areas: two large cities, Florence
        and Venice, plus San Gimignano, Monteriggioni with the Auditore
        family villa, Forlì and the Vatican, available only in the final
        mission. In Florence the cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore still
        stands unfinished; in Venice there are St. Mark’s Basilica and the
        Rialto Bridge. The scale is smaller than in today’s games, so the
        climbing doesn’t wear you out.

    body:
      - >-
        It all starts with betrayal. Ezio’s father, Giovanni, and his two
        brothers, Federico and Petruccio, are falsely accused and hanged in
        Florence. Ezio takes his mother Maria and his sister Claudia to
        Monteriggioni, to his uncle Mario, and there he learns that his
        father was an Assassin and that the Templar Order is behind the
        execution.
      - >-
        Revenge grows into an investigation lasting more than twenty years.
        Along the way come the Pazzi and their plot against Lorenzo the
        Magnificent, Venice with the Barbarigo family, Caterina Sforza in
        Forlì and the young Leonardo da Vinci, who deciphers the pages of
        Altaïr’s Codex and builds weapons from them. In 1488 Ezio obtains
        the Apple of Eden in Venice and comes face to face with the head of
        the conspiracy: Rodrigo Borgia.
      - >-
        The finale takes place in 1499 in the Vatican — by then Borgia is
        Pope Alexander VI. What Ezio finds in the vault beneath the Vatican
        changes the scale of the series: a civilization older than humanity
        appears, along with a message addressed not to him but to Desmond.
  - id: rozgrywka
    year: Mechanics
    title: Gameplay — what changed after the first game
    meta: Swimming · notoriety · the villa · two hidden blades
    art: wlochy-k03
    flip: false
    band: false
    body:
      - >-
        The core stayed: running across rooftops, viewpoints that reveal the
        map, blending into the crowd, counters in combat. What changed was
        what had been tiresome in the first game. There are about 150
        missions, two thirds of them driven by the story and the rest
        contracts and races — with no single investigation template before
        every target. Ezio swims, so water stops being a wall, and you can
        blend in with any group of people.
      - >-
        Notoriety was added: after loud actions, wanted posters go up on the
        walls and the guards watch more closely; you tear down posters,
        bribe heralds, get rid of witnesses. For florins you hire
        courtesans, thieves and mercenaries who distract, lure or fight.
        Health is restored at street doctors, and an opponent can be
        disarmed with bare hands and finished off with his own weapon.
      - >-
        From the Codex pages Leonardo makes a second hidden blade, a poison
        blade and a hidden gun; in one mission he also lends you his flying
        machine. The villa in Monteriggioni is a separate loop: investing in
        the town’s shops and buildings raises the income collected from a
        chest. Six Assassin tombs, built like levels from Prince of Persia,
        yield seals, and the full set of seals yields the Armor of Altaïr.

        hideout, where Shaun and Rebecca are waiting. The new Animus 2.0
        explains the changes in the mechanics, and the bleeding effect means
        that Desmond learns from Ezio and has flashes of Altaïr’s memories.
      - >-
        The most interesting part of this thread is not in the missions.
        Twenty glyphs are hidden in the cities, similar to the drawings
        Desmond had already seen in his cell at Abstergo; each glyph is a
        puzzle built on period paintings, and the reward for the full set is
        a short film. At the end Minerva speaks through Ezio directly to
        Desmond — and from that scene on, the modern-day story stops being
        an interlude.
  - id: dodatki-i-wydania
    year: 2010–2022
    title: DLC, editions and The Ezio Collection
    meta: The Battle of Forlì · Bonfire of the Vanities · Black Edition · Switch
    art: foto-savonarola
    flip: false
    band: true
    body:
      - >-
        The two story DLCs are sequences 12 and 13, cut from the game before
        release for lack of time and sold separately in 2010: The Battle of
        Forlì from January 28, in which Ezio defends Caterina Sforza’s city
        against the Orsi brothers, and Bonfire of the Vanities from February
        18, in which Ezio returns to the Florence of 1497, ruled by
        Savonarola. Every later release includes both.
      - >-
        The boxed editions differed in the Ezio figurine and in the number
        of extra Templar lairs: three in the Black Edition, one in the
        White; Game of the Year and the digital Deluxe collect everything.
        II was also the first game with Uplay: points unlocked Altaïr’s
        outfit and the Auditore family crypt, where it is recorded that
        Ezio’s great-grandfather was a pupil of Dante.
      - >-
        At launch the PC version had copy protection that required a
        constant internet connection; after a wave of criticism Ubisoft
        removed it, and today a one-time activation is enough. In April 2020
        II was given away for free on Uplay. The Ezio Collection — the
        trilogy with its DLC, the short film Assassin’s Creed: Lineage and
        the animated Assassin’s Creed: Embers, with refreshed graphics —
        came out in November 2016 on PlayStation 4 and Xbox One, and on
        Nintendo Switch on February 17, 2022. II has no multiplayer; that
        only arrived with Brotherhood.
  - id: jak-zaczac
    year: Start
    title: Who it’s for and how to play it today
    meta: No need for the first game · the trilogy back to back · which version
    art: wlochy-k05
    flip: true
    band: false
    body:
      - >-
        II is the classic entry point to the series and doesn’t require the
        first game: Ezio and Altaïr are 285 years apart, and Desmond’s story
        starts here with an escape that makes sense without the original.
        Brotherhood from 2010 and Revelations from 2011, on the other hand,
        are worth planning for right away — it is one story in three parts.
        Discovery on the Nintendo DS, from the same November 2009, can be
        skipped.
      - >-
        If you have the choice, go for The Ezio Collection: three games, all
        the DLC, both films, and on Switch you can also play on the go. The
        remaster didn’t change the controls or the animations — it is still
        a 2009 game in a refreshed look. The PC version on Steam has
        requirements from another era, but on new computers there are
        sometimes problems with the sound and with gamepad button prompts.
        There is also an Xbox release on Games on Demand.
      - >-
        What to expect: the main storyline is concise, and years later
        reviews keep bringing that up as a strength. Combat is simple — the
        counter wins, and against a larger group it’s better to run; the
        challenge lies in the quiet approach and in the climbing, which can
        lose the hero on the edge of a roof.
